# Thermal-point identity by canonical name

A thermal point is identified by its canonical name alone. Sector is not part of that identity, and neither is the street, which enters only as evidence resolving five labels CMTEB left ambiguous. CMTEB publishes 1,731 distinct `pt_name` strings for roughly 1,032 physical installations, varying the same name by diacritics, casing, whitespace, a ` - Partial` or ` - Module Termice` qualifier, and unqualified shorthand — mostly before 2022-07, but not only. Keying episodes on the raw `(sector, pt_name)` pair, as the derivation originally did, therefore splits single installations into several and counts them separately in every published figure.

Canonicalization is one ordered pipeline, anchored on exact matches and never a generic split on the last ` - `. The same pipeline serves the outage record and the map registry:

1. Collapse internal whitespace runs and trim.
2. Strip a trailing qualifier only by exact match against a closed list, repeating until no entry matches — ` - Partial`, ` - Module Termice`, ` - MODULE TERMICE`, ` -MODULE TERMICE`, `-module`.
3. Fold diacritics and casing.
4. Map the result through the alias table by exact match — a closed list of 25 labels, 20 keyed on the label alone and 5 on `(label, street)`.

A row whose `pt_name` is empty names no thermal point and is dropped at the same seam. Sector 6 published 20 such rows on 2026-02-03; they carry zones but nothing to attribute them to, and canonicalizing them yields the empty string, which every registry name contains. Every count of an identity under this pipeline excludes that degenerate entry, which is why each sits exactly one below the figure in the research report that established it.

**Step 2 repeats** because 16 labels stack two qualifiers (`SC 6/2 - MODULE TERMICE - Partial`, `SC.G3 - Module Termice - Partial`). A single pass strips ` - Partial`, leaves `sc 6/2 - module termice`, and never meets its own base `sc 6/2` — yielding 1,073 identities rather than 1,057.

**The closed list carries all five spellings and the trim leads rather than trails**, so one function joins both sides. The registry is what forces this: it spells the qualifier four ways, including unspaced (`SC 4/3 -MODULE TERMICE`) and truncated (`SC.2Catelu-module`), and pads 172 of its 951 names, which defeats an exact suffix match and the `MILITARI - ` prefix alike when the trim runs last. But the corpus carries those spellings too, on `SC 3/3`, `SC 4/3` and `SC.2Catelu` — each published both bare and suffixed — so a three-entry list splits three installations from themselves permanently and leaves them unjoinable to the registry, and it unstacks only 13 of the 16 stacked labels. Widening moves 1,060 → 1,057 identities and 129 → 126 identities absent from the registry, for **−1 episode, −1 incident, 0.00pp** on published figures. Leading with the trim costs nothing: it changes none of the 1,731 corpus labels, and on all 951 registry names the unified pipeline is byte-identical to the separate registry-only one it replaces.

**Step 4 folds what no rule on the label alone can reach**: shorthand, and two misspellings. Before 2022-07 CMTEB published fragments of the names it later settled on — `2 Placare` for `MILITARI - 2 Placare`, `1placare` for `1Placare-T` — and it has not stopped: `Dageco` first appears in 2024-07 for `S.C. Dageco Invest S.R.L.`, and four other fragments were still being published in 2025 or later. Beside those sit `Saboani` for `Sabaoani` and `5 Oltenitei` for `5 Oltenita`, each abandoned on the day its correctly-spelled twin appears, and neither reachable by containment: `sabaoani` does not contain `saboani`. Folding the 25 labels takes 1,057 identities to **1,032**, and 126 identities absent from the registry to **101**. It merges no registry name into another, because a label is only an alias if the registry lacks it; run over all 951 names, step 4 changes none. It does not repeat either — no alias target is itself an alias key — so it is one lookup, not a fixpoint.

**The table is a closed list, not a rule.** Unique containment against the registry is how its candidates were generated, and it is not safe to run at derivation time: five of the pre-2022-07 shorthand labels blend the two Placare estates, so containment offers two candidates and any per-label winner is wrong for half the blocks — a majority vote sends `9 Placare` to Titan 26–19 while the Mohorului, Crainicului and Valea Lungă blocks demonstrably continue under `MILITARI - 9 Placare`. Sector does not separate them either; it splits 3 of the 5. The street does, cleanly, across all 37 `(label, street)` keys, which is why those five labels are keyed on `(label, street)` and the other twenty are not. The lookup takes the street list from `zone_raw` and is order-independent in the corpus as it stands: of the 44 distinct `zone_raw` strings touching a street-scoped row, none names streets belonging to different estates. **This does not make the street part of a thermal point's identity** — it is evidence disambiguating a label CMTEB left ambiguous, consumed once at the seam, and it applies to five of the 1,057 canonical labels the earlier steps produce.

**One shorthand is deliberately left unfolded.** `Modul Termic` is contained in 84 registry names, 2,355 observations carry it, and it was still published in 2026-06 — nothing in the record says which module it means, so it stays its own identity rather than being guessed at. That is also why the `Modul Termic - X` family is not a qualifier axis: CMTEB names a module after the building it serves, and `Modul Termic - Școala nr.133` is a separate customer, not a variant of `Modul Termic`.

**A label the closed lists do not recognize stays its own identity, and only its shorthand form is worth announcing.** Had the derivation flagged every new canonical name that is a strict substring of a name already known, it would have fired 35 times in four and a half years — only 3 of them after 2022-08, one of which is real — and caught 11 of the 13 alias labels that first appear after the archive opens. It cannot catch a misspelling, which is why both misspelling rows were found by hand. The mirror check for a missed qualifier spelling — a new label whose head, up to a **spaced** hyphen, is already an identity; unspaced, it trips on the `-T` estate marker this decision spends a paragraph keeping — is pure noise: 23 fires, none of them right, all of them the `Modul Termic - X` family above. So the shorthand check earns its keep and the qualifier check does not. Which surface reports it is not decided here.

Three things that look like noise are retained, because they distinguish genuinely different installations. The **prime** marker is one: CMTEB's own [system map](https://cmteb.ro/harta_stare_sistem_termoficare_bucuresti.php) places `1/7` and `1/7'` 0.64 km apart, and `2-1 Mai` and `2’-1 Mai` 1.57 km apart, across 11 such pairs each carrying over a thousand observations on both sides. The **estate qualifiers** `-T` (Titan) and `MILITARI - ` are another: the two Placare estates reuse the numbers 1–9 and sit 10–13 km apart. **Whitespace is collapsed but never deleted**, because for the four numbers CMTEB published both ways the spacing is the whole difference between the estates — `1Placare` is the Titan point, `1 Placare` the Militari one, and step 4 keys them to different targets. For 2, 3, 7, 8 and 9 only the spaced form was ever published, and it was used for both estates, so there the spacing carries no signal and the street does the work.

Sector is excluded because it is unreliable and because CMTEB does not treat it as identity. Its map registry carries name and coordinates and no sector field at all. Of the 61 canonical identities that have appeared under more than one sector, the 58 present on that map land on **exactly one point each**, with no exceptions — including the six `Ct …` cases whose ` - Partial` row was filed under a different sector from its base, which a sector-keyed fold could never merge. Sector survives as a reported attribute (most recent label wins) and as a coarser basis level for on-time probability.

Because canonicalization changes which incidents bridge into one episode, it applies at the observation→key seam in `derive.ts`, before incidents are built — a reporting layer merging already-built episode records cannot re-run the bridge and so cannot reproduce the durations. The alias table is a checked-in constant under `src/`, beside step 2's closed list rather than under `data/`: it is a decided list that no workflow refreshes, where everything in `data/` is fetched or derived by one. `data/observations/` stays untouched as the raw archive, so the fold is revisable by re-deriving. Sector leaves the `episode_id` and `incident_id` digests, so all ids churn and every derived artifact and image is republished in one step.

The revision as a whole moves every published surface in the same direction — against CMTEB. Folding ` - Partial` alone lowers the all-history on-time rate 0.43pp with 1,057 estimates flipping hit→miss and none the other way, raises duration percentiles, and changes a third to a half of heatmap day-cells. Steps 1–3 in full fold more and drop sector: [measured end to end](../research/identity-model-delta/README.md), they merge 2,454 episodes (−1.85%) and cost 0.45pp all-history, with 1,087 estimates flipping hit→miss and none the other way — so 96.5% of the cost is ` - Partial`, and everything those steps add on top is worth 87 episodes and 0.017pp. This is the direction an accountability metric should not lean by accident, which is why the fold lands before any on-time figure is published rather than as a later correction.

**Step 4 is the exception to that direction, and costs almost nothing.** [Measured on its own](../research/alias-table-delta/README.md), it is worth **2 episodes and 0.005pp** — because it does two opposite things. The twenty label-keyed rows merge, worth −23 episodes and 11 estimates flipping hit→miss; the five `(label, street)` rows *divide* one label between two thermal points, worth +21 episodes and **5 estimates flipping miss→hit**, the only such flips anywhere in this revision. Merging lengthens the episode a claim is scored against and dividing shortens it, and the two land on disjoint claims. Fourteen of the twenty-five labels move no episode at all, `Saboani` and `5 Oltenitei` among them: a label abandoned on the day its replacement appears reunites an identity without reuniting an episode. Landing step 4 with steps 1–3 therefore costs nothing extra; landing it afterwards would be a restatement in which some figures move *up*, for a gain of 2 episodes. The whole decided model, alias table included, is **−2,434 episodes (−1.83%)** and **−0.446pp** from what is published today.

## Considered options

- **Keep `(sector, pt_name)` as the key** — no change to the derivation, but knowingly leaves 57 physical thermal points split in two on any ranking, and makes the six cross-sector ` - Partial` rows unmergeable by construction.
- **Fold all six naming axes** catalogued during research, reaching the 1,028 identities that census counted — merges the 11 prime pairs and the two Placare estates, installations up to 13 km apart, and inflates address→thermal-point resolution rates by construction.
- **Keep the closed list at the three spaced spellings** the corpus was first read as having, reaching 1,060 identities and moving no published figure at all — but the corpus does carry the unspaced and truncated forms, so this splits `SC 3/3`, `SC 4/3` and `SC.2Catelu` from their own suffixed rows permanently, leaves those three unjoinable to the registry, and keeps a second trim-first pipeline alive purely to read the registry.
- **Stop at step 3**, leaving shorthand unresolved as this decision's first three versions did — 1,057 identities, 25 of them a fragment of another, and address→thermal-point resolution drops from 97.78% to 94.36%, because a block served before 2022-07 carries both the fragment and the settled name and so resolves to two points instead of one.
- **Resolve shorthand by unique registry containment at derivation time**, no table — reaches 20 of the 25 and mis-files the other 5, whose two candidates are 10–13 km apart, and re-decides identity every time the registry is refreshed.
- **Key the ambiguous five on sector**, which is already on the observation and costs no `zone_raw` parsing — but sector splits 3 of the 5, filing 9 Titan addresses and 16 Militari ones under one label in sector 3 alone.
- **Take the alias table exactly as the address research derived it**, 22 labels — but it was derived over addresses, and `Cămin RADET`, `Ministerul de Chimie` and one Obregia module never appear at a residential address, so three installations would stay split for no reason but the table's provenance.
- **Sector as a tiebreaker on an exception list** of ambiguous names — correct on both sets, but buys a hand-curated list needing revision whenever CMTEB coins a name, to disambiguate 0.07% of observation volume.
- **Fold in the reporting layer only**, leaving `data/derived/` as published — cannot reproduce the result, because bridging is gap-sensitive and merging finished episodes does not re-run it.
- **Treat ` - Partial` as folded for aggregates but distinct for address-level reporting** — two interlocking rules where one suffices. The affected blocks live in `zone_raw`, which folding leaves untouched, so `Partial` is an attribute of the observation and address-level reporting loses nothing.
